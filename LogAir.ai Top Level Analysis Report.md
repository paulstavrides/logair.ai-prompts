# LogAir.ai Top Level Aircraft Analysis Report(tm)

Scan the logbooks for any signs of major damage or structural repairs. Specifically search for and list dates and details for:
1. Gear-up landings or prop strikes: Keywords like 'gear-up', 'belly landing', 'prop strike', 'sudden stoppage', 'dial indicator', 'crankshaft runout'.
2. Structural repairs: Keywords like 'replaced skin', 'spliced', 'bulkhead repair', 'spar replacement', 'doubler installed', 'frame alignment'.
3. Regulatory modifications: References to 'Form 337' or 'Major Repair and Alteration'.
4. Flight controls & hardware: 'rigged flight controls', 'cable tension', 'balanced control surfaces', 'fuel bladder', 'leaking tanks'.
Check citations to verify if a keyword was just an inspection or an actual repair.

Analyze the engine and propeller records. Provide a detailed summary of:
1. Times: Current SMOH, STOH, or TSN, including the date and facility of the last major overhaul.
2. Cylinders & Compressions: Any history of 'Top Overhaul', individual cylinder replacements, and the historical trend of compression checks (flag anything under 70/80 or 'exhaust valve leaking').
3. Engine Accessories: Recent maintenance on magnetos (500-hr checks), starter, alternator, fuel pump, or turbocharger.
4. Contamination: Any mentions of 'metal in screen', 'metal in filter', or 'bearing material'.
5. Propeller: SPOH, 'dressed blades', 'resealed hub', 'grease spray', or prop-strike inspections.

Analyze the operational history and maintenance cadence of this aircraft:
1. Oil Changes: Average flight hours between changes. Note if any interval exceeded 50 hours or 4 months.
2. Inactivity: Identify any gaps longer than 6 months with zero/minimal hours. Chronologically check Annual Inspections for missing years.
3. Inactivity Side-Effects: Immediately following dark periods, look for 'corrosion', 'pitting', 'camshaft/lifter replacement', or 'rust'.
4. Fleet/Harsh Usage: Search for 'pipeline', 'patrol', 'skydiving', 'jump', 'tow', 'glider', 'banner', 'agricultural', 'spray', 'flight school', or multiple short flights by different pilots on the same day.

Review the electrical, avionics, and final compliance status:
1. Certifications: Most recent dates for IFR 91.411 (static) and 91.413 (transponder) checks.
2. Upgrades: Major panel modifications (GPS, audio panels, EFIS, engine monitors removed/installed).
3. Electrical Faults: References to 'autopilot servos', 'chafed wiring', 'short circuit', or 'intermittent dropouts'.
4. Compliance & Paperwork: List complied with (C/W) Airworthiness Directives (ADs) and recurring ADs. Note any mentions of 'lost logbooks', 'duplicate logbook', or 'reconstructed records'.
